# Fluids — MCAT High-Yield Foundations

## Hydrostatic Pressure
Pressure in a static fluid increases with depth: P = ρgh. Gauge pressure excludes atmospheric pressure; absolute includes Patm. Students often forget that ρ must be in consistent SI units.

## Continuity Equation
For incompressible flow in a pipe: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂. Narrower regions mean faster flow. This is conceptual gold on the MCAT.

## Bernoulli's Principle
Along a streamline: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant. Faster flow associates with lower static pressure (not universally across all real fluids with viscosity, but ideal fluids are the MCAT default).

## Buoyancy
Archimedes: F_b = ρ_fluid × V_displaced × g. Floating objects displace fluid weight equal to object weight.

## Common Traps
- Using height of container instead of depth below surface
- Confusing volume flow rate Q = Av with velocity alone
- Ignoring that buoyant force depends on displaced fluid volume

## Reasoning Pattern
Draw a diagram, label ρ, h, and flow direction first. Ask: is the fluid static or flowing? Pick hydrostatic vs Bernoulli accordingly.
